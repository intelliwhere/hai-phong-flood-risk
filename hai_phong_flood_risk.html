Do Son
Very High — 5/5
Coastal peninsula at ~2m elevation, directly exposed to the Gulf of Tonkin. No natural barrier. Hai Phong's most typhoon-vulnerable urban zone.
Verified Incidents

Typhoon Mirinae (2009): 3m storm surge submerged the peninsula. 45,000 residents evacuated. Confirmed by ReliefWeb UN OCHA.

Coastline lost 50–80m of beach over 20 years, reducing buffer against future surges.

MARD 2021 audit rated Do Son dike systems sub-standard for Category 3+ typhoon loading.

Hai An (Port Zone)
Very High — 5/5
Hosts Hai Phong's primary container port — Vietnam's largest northern cargo gateway. At 2m elevation, port infrastructure sits in the storm surge pathway from the Van Uc river mouth.
Verified Incidents

Typhoon Son Tinh (July 2018): port access gates overtopped. 12,000 homes flooded. Operations suspended 48 hours. — VnExpress, July 2018.

River surge from Van Uc + direct coastal surge arrive simultaneously, doubling peak water levels vs. single-source events.

Tien Lang
Very High — 5/5
Hai Phong's lowest-lying district under 1.5m average elevation. First to flood when any of three river branches overflow.
Verified Incidents

2008 Red River flood: Tien Lang inundated 3–7 continuous days. 60% of farmland lost.

2017 flood: secondary dikes overtopped, district isolated. — UNDP Vietnam 2019.

UNDP projects Tien Lang loses 15–20% of its land area under a 30cm sea level rise scenario by 2050.

Duong Kinh
Very High — 5/5
Positioned between Do Son peninsula and Lach Tray river outlet — a natural funnel for converging storm surge.
Verified Incidents

Typhoon Haiyan (2013): surge propagated inland through Duong Kinh's coastal plain. MARD 2021 found dikes inadequate for 100-year return period storms.

Tidal flooding occurs 15–20 days per year during spring tides — worsening annually.

Hong Bang
High — 4/5
Historic urban core with dense development and ageing drainage. Even moderate rainfall causes significant surface flooding.
Verified Incidents

Aug 2022: 200mm in 6 hours. 40% of Hong Bang streets flooded, 1,500 homes affected. Drainage overwhelmed within 2 hours. — Tuoi Tre, August 2022.

Hong Bang designated as a priority drainage upgrade zone in Hai Phong City Flood Management Plan 2023.

Cat Hai Island
High — 4/5
Accessible only by ferry. Typhoon exposure combined with post-storm isolation creates unique risk that standard insurance does not fully capture.
Verified Incidents

Typhoon Bebinca (2024): 3,200 residents evacuated. Ferry service suspended 72 hours. All road resupply cut off. — VnExpress, 2024.

Assets cannot receive adjusters or emergency crews for 2–5 days post-typhoon. This isolation risk is not priced into standard products.

Vinh Bao
High — 4/5
Rural district at the confluence of multiple river branches. Highly vulnerable to prolonged inundation. Flooded in 7 of the last 10 years.
Verified Incidents

Oct 2017: Vinh Bao isolated for 5 days. 8,000 residents displaced. All three main access routes severed. — ReliefWeb, October 2017.

Thuy Nguyen
Medium — 3/5
Main industrial zone. Moderate physical flood risk from elevation, but elevated environmental liability risk post-flood.
Risk Basis

Physical risk: moderate — elevated terrain (5m) limits direct inundation frequency.

Liability risk: high — post-flood industrial runoff has contaminated adjacent agricultural land.

MARD baseline 2020: flagged for environmental flood risk monitoring.

How to Read This Map

Each coloured polygon represents one of Hai Phong's 15 administrative districts. The colour signals flood risk — not administrative importance or population. A small red district is more dangerous than a large yellow one.

Polygon colourReflects the combined flood risk score (1–5) derived from elevation, water proximity, typhoon history, and infrastructure ratings. Red = compound, life-threatening risk. Yellow = managed seasonal risk.
Tap any districtOpens a popup showing the verified incident that justifies the score — a real recorded event with source citation, not a model output.

Suitable vs. Unsuitable Zones

✓ Suitable

Kien An, Thuy Nguyen, An Duong — elevated terrain, manageable seasonal flood exposure. Suitable for standard development with normal insurance.

⚠ Conditional

Hong Bang, Le Chan, Ngo Quyen — urban core with drainage constraints. Viable only with flood-resilient design and infrastructure investment.

✗ Unsuitable

Do Son, Hai An, Tien Lang, Duong Kinh — direct coastal surge paths, delta flooding, or island isolation. High-value development not recommended without major mitigation.

Mitigation Priority Areas

Dike reinforcementDo Son, Duong Kinh — MARD 2021 audit rated existing systems sub-standard for Category 3+ typhoon loading.
Drainage upgradeHong Bang, Le Chan — legacy networks overwhelmed at 80mm/hr rainfall. Investment required before commercial densification.
Elevation compensationTien Lang, Vinh Bao — new construction should be raised minimum 1.5m above current grade.

For Lenders & MICs

Collateral impairment riskProperties in Very High zones carry documented flood damage histories. Values in Do Son, Hai An, and Tien Lang may not reflect latent flood liability.
LTV adjustmentStandard 70–80% LTV is inappropriate for Very High districts without flood insurance confirmation. Recommend 55–65% LTV cap.
Portfolio concentrationA lender with 30%+ of portfolio in Very High districts has correlated risk — a single typhoon can trigger simultaneous defaults across multiple assets.

For Insurers

Single-peril gapStandard flood policies cover river overflow OR storm surge — not both simultaneously. Five districts face compound events where both occur at once. This gap is not priced.
Island isolation premiumCat Hai assets require a post-event accessibility surcharge. Adjusters and repair crews cannot reach these districts for 2–5 days post-typhoon.
Industrial liability extensionThuy Nguyen floods spread industrial contaminants into adjacent agricultural land, creating third-party liability claims beyond the insured property boundary.

For Developers

Design standardsConstruction in High or Very High zones should reference Vietnam MARD flood resilience guidelines. Raised foundations and flood-resistant materials are non-negotiable in red zones.
Permit riskVietnam's National Climate Change Strategy (2021) signals tightening development controls in coastal flood zones. Projects approved today face elevated permit renewal risk over a 20-year horizon.

🏙️ Hai Phong City Context
×

Why Hai Phong Floods

Hai Phong sits at the mouth of the Red River Delta, where Vietnam's largest river system meets the Gulf of Tonkin. Most urban areas sit just 1–4 metres above sea level. Three separate flood mechanisms converge simultaneously:

Typhoons — The Gulf of Tonkin is one of Asia's most active typhoon corridors. Storms funnel directly toward Hai Phong's coast.
River overflow — The Thai Binh and Van Uc rivers drain the entire northern highlands and back up into low-lying districts after monsoon rain.
Storm surge — Typhoon winds push seawater inland. A 1m surge, typical in a Category 2 storm, inundates most of the urban core.

Scale of Historical Impact

14 significant typhoons within 100km of Hai Phong between 1990 and 2024 (NOAA IBTrACS)
45,000 evacuated during Typhoon Mirinae (2009) — Do Son peninsula submerged under 3m surge
12,000 homes flooded in Hai An during Typhoon Son Tinh (2018) — port gates overtopped
$4M+ USD crop loss in An Lao when Thai Binh River dikes failed 2017 (FAO Vietnam)

Climate Trajectory

Sea level rise of 20–30cm by 2050 projected for the Red River Delta coast (UNDP Vietnam 2019)
Typhoon intensification: storms reaching higher wind speeds faster due to warmer Gulf of Tonkin sea temperatures
Monsoon rainfall increasingly concentrated into shorter, more intense events — overwhelming legacy drainage systems
