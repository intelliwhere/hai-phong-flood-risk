Hai Phong Flood Context

Why Hai Phong Floods

Hai Phong sits at the mouth of the Red River Delta, where Vietnam's largest river system meets the Gulf of Tonkin. Most urban areas sit just 1 to 4 metres above sea level.

Three separate flood mechanisms converge simultaneously:

Typhoons — The Gulf of Tonkin is one of Asia's most active typhoon corridors. Storms funnel directly toward Hai Phong's coast.
River overflow — The Thai Binh and Van Uc rivers drain the entire northern highlands and back up into low-lying districts after monsoon rain.
Storm surge — Typhoon winds push seawater inland. A 1m surge, typical in a Category 2 storm, inundates most of the urban core.

Scale of Historical Impact

14 significant typhoons within 100km of Hai Phong between 1990 and 2024 (NOAA IBTrACS)
45,000 evacuated during Typhoon Mirinae (2009) — Do Son peninsula submerged under 3m surge
12,000 homes flooded in Hai An during Typhoon Son Tinh (2018) — port gates overtopped
8,000 displaced in Vinh Bao (2017) — isolated 5 days, no road access
$4M+ USD crop loss in An Lao when Thai Binh River dikes failed 2017 (FAO Vietnam)

Climate Trajectory

Sea level rise of 20 to 30cm by 2050 projected for the Red River Delta coast (UNDP Vietnam 2019)
Typhoon intensification: storms reaching higher wind speeds faster due to warmer Gulf of Tonkin sea temperatures
Monsoon rainfall increasingly concentrated into shorter, more intense events — overwhelming legacy drainage systems

Implications for Asset Risk

Collateral impairment — Properties in Very High zones have documented damage histories. Values may not reflect latent flood liability.
Business interruption — Hai An port suspended during events. Thuy Nguyen industrial zones face post-flood contamination liability.
Insurance gap — Standard single-peril policies may not cover compound typhoon + surge + river overflow events simultaneously.
Evacuation isolation — Cat Hai island cut off 72+ hours post-typhoon. No road evacuation corridor exists.

District Score Reasoning ✕
Do Son District
Very High — Score 5/5

Coastal peninsula at ~2m elevation, directly exposed to the Gulf of Tonkin. No natural barrier. Hai Phong's most typhoon-vulnerable urban zone.

Verified Incidents

Typhoon Mirinae (2009): 3m storm surge submerged the peninsula. 45,000 residents evacuated. Confirmed by ReliefWeb UN OCHA situation report.

Coastline lost 50-80m of beach over 20 years, reducing buffer against future surges.

MARD 2021 audit rated Do Son dike systems sub-standard for Category 3+ typhoon loading.

Hai An District (Port Zone)
Very High — Score 5/5

Hosts Hai Phong's primary container port — Vietnam's largest northern cargo gateway. At 2m elevation, port infrastructure sits in the storm surge pathway from the Van Uc river mouth.

Verified Incidents

Typhoon Son Tinh (July 2018): port access gates overtopped. 12,000 homes flooded. Operations suspended 48 hours. — VnExpress, July 2018.

River surge from Van Uc + direct coastal surge arrive simultaneously, doubling peak water levels vs. single-source events.

Tien Lang District
Very High — Score 5/5

Hai Phong's lowest-lying district under 1.5m average elevation. First to flood when any of three river branches overflow.

Verified Incidents

2008 Red River flood: Tien Lang inundated 3-7 continuous days. 60% of farmland lost.

2017 flood: secondary dikes overtopped, district isolated. — UNDP Vietnam 2019 climate displacement report.

UNDP projects Tien Lang loses 15-20% of its land area under a 30cm sea level rise scenario by 2050.

Duong Kinh District
Very High — Score 5/5

Positioned between Do Son peninsula and Lach Tray river outlet — a natural funnel for converging storm surge.

Verified Incidents

Typhoon Haiyan (2013): surge propagated inland through Duong Kinh's coastal plain. MARD 2021 audit found dikes inadequate for 100-year return period storms.

Tidal flooding occurs 15-20 days per year during high spring tides — worsening annually per local government flood reports.

Hong Bang District
High — Score 4/5

Historic urban core with dense development and ageing drainage infrastructure. Even moderate rainfall events cause significant surface flooding.

Verified Incidents

August 2022: 200mm in 6 hours. 40% of Hong Bang streets flooded, 1,500 homes affected. Drainage overwhelmed within 2 hours of storm onset. — Tuoi Tre, August 2022.

Hong Bang designated as a priority drainage upgrade zone in Hai Phong City Flood Management Plan 2023.

Cat Hai Island District
High — Score 4/5

Populated island accessible only by ferry. Typhoon exposure combined with post-storm isolation creates unique, hard-to-price asset risk that standard insurance does not fully capture.

Verified Incidents

Typhoon Bebinca (2024): 3,200 residents evacuated. Ferry service suspended 72 hours. All road resupply cut off. — VnExpress, 2024.

Assets on Cat Hai cannot receive emergency crews or insurance adjusters for 2-5 days post-typhoon. This isolation risk is not priced into standard insurance products.

Vinh Bao District
High — Score 4/5

Rural district at the confluence of multiple river branches. Highly vulnerable to prolonged inundation. Floods in 7 of the last 10 years.

Verified Incidents

October 2017 flood: Vinh Bao isolated for 5 days. 8,000 residents displaced. Road access severed on all three main approach routes. — ReliefWeb, October 2017.

Thuy Nguyen District
Medium — Score 3/5

Main industrial zone with steel mills and heavy manufacturing. Moderate physical flood risk from elevation, but elevated environmental liability risk post-flood.

Risk Basis

Physical risk: moderate — elevated terrain (5m) limits direct inundation frequency.

Liability risk: high — post-flood industrial runoff has contaminated adjacent agricultural land, creating liability well beyond the flood zone.

MARD baseline 2020: flagged for environmental flood risk monitoring.

How to Read This Map

Each coloured polygon represents one of Hai Phong's 15 administrative districts. The colour and size signal flood risk — not administrative importance or population. A small red district is more dangerous than a large yellow one.

Polygon ColourReflects the combined flood risk score (1–5) derived from elevation, water proximity, typhoon history, and infrastructure ratings. Red = compound, life-threatening risk. Yellow = managed seasonal risk.

Suitable vs. Unsuitable Zones

✓ Suitable Kien An, Thuy Nguyen, An Duong — elevated terrain, manageable seasonal flood exposure. Suitable for standard commercial or residential development with normal insurance.

⚠ Conditional Hong Bang, Le Chan, Ngo Quyen — urban core districts with drainage constraints. Development viable only with flood-resilient design and infrastructure investment.

✕ Unsuitable Do Son, Hai An, Tien Lang, Duong Kinh, Bach Long Vi — direct coastal surge paths, delta flooding, or island isolation. High-value fixed asset development is not recommended without major mitigation works.

Areas Requiring Mitigation

Dike Reinforcement PriorityDo Son, Duong Kinh — MARD 2021 audit rated existing dike systems sub-standard for Category 3+ typhoon loading. Any development here requires confirmed dike upgrade as a precondition.
Drainage Upgrade PriorityHong Bang, Le Chan — legacy drainage networks overwhelmed at 80mm/hr rainfall. Infrastructure investment required before commercial densification.
Elevation CompensationTien Lang, Vinh Bao — new construction should be raised minimum 1.5m above current grade. Standard slab-on-grade is inappropriate.

Relative Safety Within Districts

Even within High-risk districts, not all land carries equal exposure. Northern and elevated sub-areas of Hong Bang and Ngo Quyen have meaningfully lower inundation frequency than their southern waterfront zones. Site-specific elevation surveys are recommended before any parcel-level decisions.

📌 Risk Implications ✕

For Lenders & MICs

Collateral Impairment RiskProperties in Very High zones carry documented flood damage histories. Market values in Do Son, Hai An, and Tien Lang may not fully reflect latent flood liability — particularly for assets acquired before 2018.
LTV AdjustmentStandard 70–80% LTV is inappropriate for Very High districts without flood insurance confirmation. Recommend 55–65% LTV cap until site-specific mitigation is confirmed.
Portfolio ConcentrationA lender with 30%+ of portfolio in Very High districts has correlated risk — a single major typhoon can trigger simultaneous defaults across multiple assets.

For Insurers

Single-Peril GapStandard flood policies cover river overflow OR storm surge — not both simultaneously. Five Hai Phong districts face compound events where both occur at once. This gap is not priced into standard products.
Island Isolation PremiumCat Hai and Bach Long Vi assets require a post-event accessibility surcharge. Adjusters, repair crews, and emergency response cannot reach these districts for 2–5 days post-typhoon.
Industrial Liability ExtensionThuy Nguyen floods spread industrial contaminants into adjacent agricultural land, creating third-party liability claims well beyond the insured property boundary.

For Developers

Design StandardsAny construction in High or Very High zones should reference Vietnam MARD flood resilience guidelines as a minimum. Raised foundations, flood-resistant materials, and emergency egress planning are non-negotiable in red zones.
Permit RiskVietnam's National Climate Change Strategy (2021) signals tightening development controls in coastal flood zones. Projects approved today in red districts face elevated permit renewal risk over a 20-year horizon.

For Local Government

Evacuation PlanningCat Hai and Bach Long Vi require pre-positioned emergency supplies — ferry cutoffs of 72+ hours mean post-event resupply cannot be the primary strategy.
Investment SequencingDike upgrades in Do Son and drainage investment in Hong Bang should precede any densification approvals in those districts. Infrastructure leads, development follows.
👥 Client Recommendations ✕

For Insurers

Immediate Actions

•  Require compound flood cover (surge + river overflow) for all new commercial policies in Very High districts
•  Add Cat Hai and Bach Long Vi island isolation rider to all property policies in those districts
•  Commission site-specific elevation surveys for all policies above $500K USD exposure in red zones

Portfolio Strategy

Apply a 25–35% premium loading in Very High districts versus equivalent assets in Medium districts. Tien Lang and Duong Kinh are currently underpriced relative to documented loss history.

For Developers

Site Selection Priority Order

1. Kien An & Thuy Nguyen — lowest flood exposure, industrial/commercial suitable
2. An Duong — rural fringe, residential development viable with standard drainage
3. Ngo Quyen — urban core, viable with drainage investment and raised construction
4. Avoid Very High districts for fixed asset development without major dike works

Due Diligence Checklist

✓ Obtain MARD dike adequacy rating for the specific parcel
✓ Confirm flood insurance availability and compound cover
✓ Review Conservation Authority / MONRE flood extent maps at 1:5,000 scale
✓ Verify finished floor elevation relative to 100-year flood level

For Lenders / MICs

Underwriting Policy Adjustments

•  Very High districts: require flood insurance confirmation + independent appraisal before approval
•  High districts: flood risk disclosure to borrower required; cap LTV at 65%
•  Medium districts: standard underwriting with annual flood risk review
•  Portfolio: flag any concentration above 20% in any single risk tier

For Local Government

Infrastructure Investment Priority

1. Do Son & Duong Kinh dike system upgrade — highest return on risk reduction
2. Hong Bang & Le Chan drainage modernisation — addresses recurrent urban flooding
3. Cat Hai pre-positioned emergency supply depot — closes 72hr ferry cutoff gap
4. Tien Lang managed retreat planning — lowest elevation district, highest long-term sea level risk
